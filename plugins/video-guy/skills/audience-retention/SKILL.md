# Audience retention

Retention is a structural property, not a polish pass. You cannot edit it in
later and you cannot narrate it in.

## The first thirty seconds

Most of the audience that leaves, leaves here. The open must:

- start on something **concrete** — an image, an object, a sentence somebody
  wrote at the time;
- open a question the viewer now wants answered;
- promise the film's actual subject, honestly;
- do all of it before any context, any channel branding, any "in this video".

Diagnostics: if the first sentence would work as the video's own summary, it is
a summary and it will not hold. If the first fifteen seconds are orientation,
the hook is at 0:15 and everything before it is a cost.

## Open loops

Keep a ledger: every question, where it opens, where it pays off.

- At least one question open at every moment.
- Small questions pay off within 60–90 seconds.
- One big question may run the film's length; only one.
- Every opened question pays off. An unpaid loop is remembered as a broken
  promise even when the viewer cannot name it.

## Where films die

1. **Context before curiosity.** Two minutes of background at 1:30 with nothing
   at stake. The single most common failure.
2. **Flat escalation.** Beats that do not change the situation read as a list.
3. **The mid-film sag** — after the setup pays off and before the crisis. Fix by
   opening a new question at the moment the old one closes.
4. **Visual monotony.** Attention drifts even when the words are good; a stretch
   of long static holds is a nap regardless of the narration.
5. **The premature summary.** Restating what the viewer just learned tells them
   they are done.

## Pacing is a curve, not a speed

Vary it deliberately: fast for escalation, slow for consequence. Uniform pacing
is fatiguing at any speed. Silence before the film's most important sentence
does more than any sound effect.

## Chapters and thumbnails are part of retention

The title and thumbnail set the promise; the cold open must pay it in the first
fifteen seconds. A hook that does not match the thumbnail loses the exact
audience the thumbnail attracted.

## Honest checks

- Read the section list alone. Would you watch it?
- Find the first 60-second window with no open question.
- Watch the cut at 2× — the boring stretches survive the speed-up, which is
  what makes them findable.
